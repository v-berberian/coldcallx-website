Free vs paid power dialer: the honest cost math

Cold Call X · Published August 28, 2026 · 8 min read

Search “free auto dialer” and you get three kinds of answers: freemium apps with hard caps, sales platforms with a free trial and a real price behind the demo wall, and comparison posts written by vendors who all happen to rank themselves #1. This guide skips the spin: what free plans actually include, what paid options really cost (with verified, dated numbers), and how to compute your own break-even as a solo caller.

Full disclosure, stated up front: we make Cold Call X, a $12.99 one-time iPhone power dialer with no subscription. Every competitor price below is quoted from a public source with a check date, so you can do the math yourself even if you never buy from us.

What “free” dialer plans actually give you

Free tiers are real products, not scams — they are just sized so that anyone calling seriously outgrows them. Two current examples, checked August 28, 2026:

  • GoDial — its App Store listing states the app is free for up to 100 contacts and 30 calls per day, with a subscription above that (checked Aug 28, 2026, apps.apple.com).
  • Calley — publishes a free “Personal” plan it describes as free forever, positioned around bulk CSV loading through a web panel (checked Aug 28, 2026, getcalley.com).

What free plans typically leave out: large or unlimited lists, CSV/Sheets import with automatic mapping, filters (called/uncalled, time zone, temperature), tags and notes that persist across sessions, follow-up scheduling, and export. The dollar cost is zero; the cost in manual workarounds is not. If you spend 20 minutes a day re-copying numbers into the phone app, a free tool is costing you more than it saves.

Rule of thumb: a free plan is the right tool when you call occasionally from a short list. Once you dial daily from hundreds of contacts, the caps — not the features — become the bottleneck.

What paid dialers actually cost (verified, dated)

Recurring dialer pricing splits into three shapes. All figures were verified at the cited sources on August 28, 2026; pricing changes, so treat these as snapshots and confirm before purchase.

1. Mobile subscriptions

SwiftCall (iPhone) is listed at $9.99 on the App Store right now — but that is a limited-time sale (“over 80% off, save $50” banner); the regular price is $65, and its Pro subscription runs $34.99/month (checked Aug 27, 2026, apps.apple.com). Sales prices are the oldest trick in this category: always check whether the headline number is the real price.

2. À la carte real estate platforms

Mojo charges $10/month per user for access, plus $89/month for a single-line dialer or $139 for triple-line, with recording ($25), voice ($30) and caller ID ($10) as add-ons — a solo agent realistically starts around $99–$214/month (checked Aug 2026, mojosells.com; details in our Mojo Dialer alternative guide).

3. Team sales platforms

PhoneBurner runs $140 / $165 / $183 per user per month on its annual tiers (Standard/Professional/Premium; monthly billing is higher) per CloudTalk's August 2026 pricing guide, cross-checked against PhoneBurner's own pricing page — which now renders tiers client-side, so verify there before buying (checked Aug 28, 2026). Break-even question: would you notice if your dialer disappeared tomorrow, other than the dialing? If the answer is “I'd just retype my list,” you don't need a monthly platform. Checklist before you pay anyone

  • Is the headline price the real price? Check for sale banners and expiring discounts (see SwiftCall above).
  • What does calling actually cost? VoIP minutes, DID numbers, recording and caller-ID add-ons can double a base price.
  • Per seat or per line? À la carte platforms price lines separately from users.
  • What are the free tier's caps? Contacts, calls per day, import formats.
  • Where does your lead data live? On-device vs vendor cloud is a real difference — check the privacy label, not the marketing page.

Bottom line

Free plans are genuinely useful until caps bite. Paid subscriptions are genuinely worth it for teams.

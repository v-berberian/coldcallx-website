Free vs paid power dialer: the honest cost math

By Vadym Berberian · Cold Call X · Updated September 20, 2026 · 9 min read

Search “free auto dialer” and you get three kinds of answers: freemium apps with hard caps, sales platforms with a free trial and a real price behind the demo wall, and comparison posts written by vendors who all happen to rank themselves #1. This guide skips the spin: what free plans actually include, what paid options really cost (with verified, dated numbers), and how to compute your own break-even as a solo caller.

Full disclosure, stated up front: we make Cold Call X, a $12.99 one-time iPhone power dialer with no subscription. Every competitor price below is quoted from a public source with a check date, so you can do the math yourself even if you never buy from us.

What “free” dialer plans actually give you

Free tiers are real products, not scams; they are just sized so that anyone calling seriously outgrows them. Two current examples, checked September 16, 2026:

  • GoDial, its App Store listing states the app is free for up to 100 contacts and 30 calls per day, with a subscription above that (checked Sep 20, 2026, apps.apple.com).
  • Calley, publishes a free “Personal” plan ($0.00, no expiry) that caps calls at 25 per day with a single calling list (checked Sep 20, 2026, getcalley.com).

What free plans typically leave out: large or unlimited lists, CSV/Sheets import with automatic mapping, filters (called/uncalled, time zone, temperature), tags and notes that persist across sessions, follow-up scheduling, and export. The dollar cost is zero; the cost in manual workarounds is not. If you spend 20 minutes a day re-copying numbers into the phone app, a free tool is costing you more than it saves.

Rule of thumb: a free plan is the right tool when you call occasionally from a short list. Once you dial daily from hundreds of contacts, the caps, not the features, become the bottleneck.

What paid dialers actually cost (verified, dated)

Recurring dialer pricing splits into three shapes. All figures were verified at the cited sources on their individual check dates below; pricing changes, so treat these as snapshots and confirm before purchase.

1. Mobile subscriptions

SwiftCall (iPhone) is listed at $9.99 on the App Store (checked Sep 20, 2026), but an August 27, 2026 check found that price behind an “over 80% off, save $50” sale banner; the regular price is $65 (confirmed August 27, 2026), and its Pro subscription runs $34.99/month (verified September 20, 2026). Sale prices are the oldest trick in this category: always check whether the headline number is the real price.

2. À la carte real estate platforms

Mojo charges $10/month per user for access, plus $89/month for a single-line dialer or $139 for triple-line, with recording ($25), voice ($30) and caller ID ($10) as add-ons, a solo agent realistically starts around $99–$214/month (checked Sep 20, 2026, mojosells.com; details in our Mojo Dialer alternative guide).

3. Team sales platforms

PhoneBurner runs $140 / $165 / $183 per user per month on its annual tiers (Standard/Professional/Premium; monthly billing is higher) per CloudTalk's pricing guide, re-confirmed via 2026 sources and PhoneBurner's own pricing page, which renders tiers client-side ($165 Professional annual, $195 monthly at our September 20, 2026 check). Break-even question: would you notice if your dialer disappeared tomorrow, other than the dialing? If the answer is “I'd just retype my list,” you don't need a monthly platform. If the answer involves five reps, shared reporting, and voicemail drops at scale, you do, and the per-seat price is buying something real.

Compare the workflow before the price

A low monthly price is not automatically a low total cost, and a one-time price is not automatically the right fit. Start with the workflow: a solo broker working a personally owned list needs reliable import, a calling sequence, notes, callbacks, and a way to keep opt-outs from returning to the queue. A branch that needs shared ownership, manager reporting, coaching, or CRM routing needs a team platform, even when the per-seat math is higher.

  • Choose a free tier when a short list and hard daily caps are genuinely enough.
  • Choose a one-time mobile app when one person needs to work a list from their own phone without adding a recurring software bill.
  • Choose team software when the work depends on shared records, roles, integrations, or multi-agent management.

Checklist before you pay anyone

  • Is the headline price the real price? Check for sale banners and expiring discounts (see SwiftCall above).
  • What does calling actually cost? VoIP minutes, DID numbers, recording and caller-ID add-ons can double a base price.
  • Per seat or per line? À la carte platforms price lines separately from users.
  • What are the free tier's caps? Contacts, calls per day, import formats.
  • Where does your lead data live? On-device vs vendor cloud is a real difference, check the privacy label, not the marketing page.

Bottom line

Free plans are genuinely useful until caps bite. Paid subscriptions are genuinely worth it for teams. The gap in between, a solo caller who just wants their list dialed in order from their own phone, is exactly where a one-time-purchase tool makes the arithmetic boring: $12.99 once versus $1,200–$1,700 a year for the platforms priced above. Dialer costs, answered

Are there free auto dialer apps that actually work?+

Yes, with caps. GoDial's listing describes free use up to 100 contacts and 30 calls/day; Calley publishes a free Personal plan. Serious daily volume usually outgrows free tiers.

How much does a paid power dialer cost per month?+

Verified September 2026: CloudTalk runs $19–$25/user/month annually for its lower tiers, with Expert at $49 and power-dialer features; Mojo's stack starts around $99/month solo before add-ons; PhoneBurner runs $140–$183 per user per month on annual billing. Team platforms add VoIP per-seat costs.

CloudTalk's published 2026 pricing lists Lite at $19/user/month annually, Starter at $25, and Expert at $49; the Expert tier is the one associated with power-dialer features. Confirm the current plan and billing terms before buying.

Is a one-time purchase dialer cheaper than a subscription?+

For solo callers, yes over any meaningful horizon: Cold Call X is $12.99 once, while a single month of PhoneBurner's Standard annual tier costs over ten times that. Subscriptions buy team features you may not need.

What do free dialer plans usually leave out?+

Contact and daily-call caps, bulk import, filters, persistent tags and notes, and export. The cost shows up as manual workarounds instead of a price tag.

What hidden costs should I check before choosing a dialer?+

VoIP minute charges, extra numbers, recording/caller-ID add-ons, per-seat vs per-line pricing, and limited-time sale prices presented as the regular price.

Should a solo broker pay for team dialer software?+

Only if the workflow needs its team features. A solo broker may only need import, calling, notes, callbacks, and opt-out suppression. Shared records, manager reporting, coaching, CRM routing, and multi-agent administration are reasons to pay for a team platform.

How should I compare a free tier, a one-time app, and a subscription?+

Compare the limits and workflow first, then the total cost. Free tiers fit short, occasional lists with hard caps. A one-time mobile app fits one person working a list from their own phone. A subscription is worth it when the work requires shared records, roles, integrations, or multi-agent management.

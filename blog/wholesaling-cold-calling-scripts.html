Wholesaling cold calling scripts that get contracts signed

Cold Call X · Published August 2026 · 9 min read

Wholesaling runs on motivated sellers, and motivated sellers are found on the phone. The math is blunt: most lists surface one true motivated seller per 30–50 dials, so the wholesaling cold calling scripts below are built for volume and honesty at once — a core opener that works on any list, situational versions for probate, absentee owners and tired landlords, the objection handlers that keep a maybe alive, and the voicemail and text that make the second touch land. Every script assumes you're calling live, one homeowner at a time, from your own number — which also happens to be the most compliance-cautious way to dial.

The core wholesaling opener

Wholesaling calls fail in the first eight seconds when the seller hears "investor" and pictures a robocall house-flipper. The fix: sound like a neighbor with a reason. Lead with the property, not your business.

Hi, is this Sarah? Hey Sarah — this is Mike Jones, I'm a local homebuyer here in Springfield. The reason I'm calling — and I know this is out of the blue — I buy a couple of houses a year on your side of town, and I had a couple of questions about the property on Maple. Are you the owner?

Why it works: "a couple of houses a year" signals small and local, not a call center. "I know this is out of the blue" disarms instead of apologizing. And the close — are you the owner — is an easy yes that starts the conversation instead of a pitch that ends it. If you're working expired listings as a wholesaler or investor rather than an agent, use the reframed openers in the expired listing scripts guide — same structure, agent-appropriate wording.

The situation scripts

Probate and inherited property

The most sensitive list you'll ever dial. The seller just lost someone and is now fielding vulture calls. Slow down, name the situation once, and offer an exit — never push.

Hi, is this David? David, this is Mike Jones — I'm a local buyer here in Springfield. I'll be direct about why I'm calling: records show you've recently become the owner of the home on Cedar, and I often work with families in that situation who'd rather sell as-is than take on an estate property. If that's not where you are, no problem at all — I'm sorry for what prompted the call. But if it is something you'd want a simple way out of, I buy homes in exactly that condition and can close on your timeline.

Two rules: never ask how the person died or volunteer sympathy you don't feel — one respectful acknowledgment is enough — and never fake a relationship with the deceased. Probate lists out-convert everything else precisely because the motivation is structural, not emotional.

Absentee owners (out-of-state landlords)

These sellers don't live in the headache. Your job is to remind them the headache exists and that distance is the problem.

Hi, this is Mike Jones — I'm calling about the rental you own on Elm here in Springfield. I work with out-of-area owners who are tired of managing a property from a distance, and I buy homes as-is, tenants and all, so there's nothing to clean, fix, or coordinate. Is keeping that property long-distance still working for you, or would it be worth hearing what it'd sell for today?

"Tenants and all" is the hook — it removes the eviction-and-repair objection before it forms. Pair this list with the landlord framing in the full real estate cold calling scripts guide.

Tired landlords (in-town)

Hi, is this Maria? Maria, Mike Jones here — local buyer in Springfield. Quick question and I'll let you go: are you still renting out the place on Brookside, or have you ever thought about just cashing out of it? I ask because I buy rental properties directly, as-is, and a lot of owners I talk to are one bad tenant story away from being done. Where are you at with it?

"One bad tenant story away from being done" is a mirror, not a pitch. Landlords laugh at that line — then tell you the story. That story is your deal.

Pre-foreclosure / notice of default

The highest-stakes list. Be the calm option, never the fear-monger, and never imply you represent the bank or the government.

Hi, is this James? James, this is Mike Jones, I'm a local home buyer. I'll be straightforward — public records indicate there's a notice of default on the property on Ash, and I work with homeowners in that situation to find the fastest way out, whether that's a cash sale that stops the clock or simply knowing the options. I'm not a bank or attorney and this isn't official anything — but if it would help to know what an as-is cash offer looks like with a closing date you choose, I can have that to you today.

Driving for dollars follow-up

Hi, is this Robert? Robert — Mike Jones here, local buyer. I was actually driving your street last week, Glenview, and noticed the place on the corner — I drive neighborhoods looking for homes I might buy directly from owners, and that one caught my eye. Any chance you've thought about selling it somewhere down the road, even if not today?

"Even if not today" is the whole play. Driving-for-dollars leads are future deals; tag them warm and call back in 90 days. For agent-side circle prospecting variants, see the circle prospecting scripts.

The objection handlers

"How much are you offering?"

Never quote blind — you'll be wrong in one direction or the other. Trade numbers for information:

Honestly, I'd be guessing if I threw a number out right now, and I don't want to do that. Here's what I can do: fifteen minutes looking at the property and the numbers, and I'll give you a firm cash offer in writing, good for 30 days, no obligation. Fair enough?

"Are you an agent?"

No — I'm not an agent and there's no commission involved. I buy houses directly, or I work with a small group of local buyers. If at any point selling with an agent makes more sense for you, I'll actually tell you that.

That last sentence converts. Sellers have been burned by investors who pretend an agent is a scam; being the buyer who recommends a realtor when it fits wins the deals that matter.

"I'm not interested."

Totally fair — you didn't expect my call. One thing before I let you go: if the situation ever changes and you want a no-hassle cash number for the place, save my number. Mind if I check back in a few months to see how you're doing?

A polite no plus a scheduled callback is a pipeline, not a loss. The fortune in wholesaling is in the fourth call, not the first.

"Who is this / how did you get my number?"

That's a fair question — property ownership is public record, and your name came up attached to the home on Maple. I buy a few houses a year in the area and reach out to owners directly. If this isn't a good time, I can call back when it works better.

Never hide the source. Homeowners already assume public records; honesty about it sounds like professionalism rather than surveillance.

The wholesaler's voicemail

Most wholesaling dials roll to voicemail. Keep it under 25 seconds, lead with the property, and give an easy out — the full playbook is in the real estate voicemail scripts guide, but the wholesaler version sounds like this:

Hi Sarah — Mike Jones, local homebuyer here in Springfield, calling about the house on Maple. I buy a couple of homes a year on that side of town and would make a fair cash offer, as-is, if you'd ever consider selling. No pressure either way — my number is 555-0100. Again, 555-0100. Thanks, Sarah.

The follow-up text

Where you have consent or the seller's number was published for contact purposes, a two-sentence text an hour after the call keeps you findable without a second interruption:

Hi Sarah — Mike Jones, the local buyer who called about your place on Maple. If you'd ever want a no-obligation cash offer, just reply OFFER and I'll put one together today.

Staying on the right side of the rules

Wholesaling attracts regulatory attention, so the dialing side deserves the same discipline as the scripts. In broad strokes — this is general information, not legal advice, and the rules are in flux:

  • Dial live, one call at a time. Federal TCPA rules restrict equipment that generates numbers randomly or sequentially and prerecorded messages. Calling a list you compiled, speaking live from your own phone, is the cautious lane. A manual dialer app that simply walks your imported list in order — never generating or guessing numbers — keeps the human in the loop.
  • Scrub the Do Not Call Registry. Numbers on the National DNC Registry are generally off-limits for telemarketing; an internal suppression list (a do-not-call list you build as people ask) is required alongside it. Cold Call X ships with a built-in suppression list for exactly this.
  • Identify yourself honestly. Your name, your purpose, no fake buyers, no "I have a cash offer ready" theater.
  • State disclosure duties. Many states require wholesalers to disclose the intent to assign the contract; know your state's rules before you sign, not after.
  • Call at reasonable hours. Federal telemarketing rules restrict calls to roughly 8 a.m.–9 p.m. local time — and best-answer windows are tighter: the data is in the best time to cold call. For the full rule-by-rule breakdown, see are auto dialers legal? TCPA auto dialer laws explained.

Quick takeaways

  • Lead with the property, not the word "investor." Ask if they're the owner.
  • Probate: acknowledge once, offer the exit, never push. Absentee: "tenants and all." Tired landlord: "one bad tenant story."
  • Never quote blind — trade a firm written offer for fifteen minutes of access.
  • Voicemail under 25 seconds; one consent-safe follow-up text with one-word reply mechanics.
  • Dial live, scrub DNC, keep your own suppression list, disclose assignment intent per your state.

Wholesaling scripts, answered

Is cold calling legal for real estate wholesalers?+

Yes — live, one-at-a-time calls from your own phone are legal prospecting. TCPA rules restrict auto-generating numbers, prerecorded messages, and calling Do Not Call Registry numbers. Dial your own list live and scrub against DNC. Not legal advice.

How many cold calls does it take to get a wholesale deal?+

Commonly cited benchmarks: ~1 motivated-seller conversation per 30–50 dials, with a signed contract typically taking several hundred to a couple thousand dials. List quality (probate, pre-foreclosure, absentee) moves the math more than volume.

Should a wholesaler say they are an investor on the call?+

Be honest but plain: "I buy houses in the area" or "I work with local buyers." Never claim to be the end cash buyer if you intend to assign, never imply you're an agent, and disclose your intent to resell the contract where state law requires it.

What is the best list for wholesaling cold calls?+

Probate and inherited homes, pre-foreclosure/notice-of-default, out-of-state landlords, tired in-town landlords, expired listings, and high-equity absentee owners. Distress plus distance equals motivation.

Do voicemail drops work for wholesaling?+

They save time and lose deals: sellers recognize prerecorded drops in seconds, and prerecorded messages to mobiles without consent carry strict per-call TCPA penalties. Leave a short live voicemail instead.

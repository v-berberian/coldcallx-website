Circle prospecting scripts for real estate

Cold Call X · Updated August 2026 · 8 min read

A new listing, open house, pending sale, or sold sign gives you a real reason to call the nearby homeowners. That is the useful part of circle prospecting: you are not inventing a vague “just checking in” reason. You are sharing a local event and asking whether it matters to them.

Compliance first: A relevant reason does not erase telemarketing rules. Follow federal and state Do Not Call requirements, your brokerage policy, and applicable calling-hour rules. Maintain your own suppression list, identify yourself, honor “do not call” immediately, and get advice from a qualified attorney for your situation. This guide is general information, not legal advice.

What makes a circle call work?

The first ten seconds should answer three questions: who are you, why this street, and what is in it for the homeowner? Then stop talking. The best circle calls sound like a neighborly market update, not a memorized pitch.

  • Use one trigger. Pick just listed, open house, under contract, or just sold.
  • Lead with a fact. Say the address and the event instead of “I’m calling about your real estate needs.”
  • Ask one question. “Curious what that means for your value?” is easier to answer than a string of qualifying questions.
  • Offer a small next step. A two-minute value range or buyer-match conversation is less demanding than asking for a listing appointment immediately.

Four circle prospecting scripts

1. Just-listed script

“Hi, is this {Name}? This is {Your Name} with {Brokerage}. I’m calling because I just listed {Address} nearby. I’m letting the closest neighbors know before the open house, do you know anyone who has been waiting for a home in this area?”

If they say no: “No problem. I also wanted to ask, are you curious what a new listing at that price could mean for your own home’s value?”

2. Open-house invitation

“Hi {Name}, {Your Name} with {Brokerage}. I’m hosting an open house at {Address} on {Day} from {Time}. Since you’re nearby, I wanted to invite you personally. Would you like me to text or email the details, or would you rather stop by?”

Do not turn an invitation into a forced valuation pitch. If they attend, ask what they noticed and whether they want a private conversation afterward.

3. Under-contract script

“Hi {Name}, this is {Your Name} with {Brokerage}. The home at {Address} just went under contract, and I’m calling a few nearby owners because that can change the picture for buyers still looking in the neighborhood. Are you hearing from anyone who needs a home like yours?”

The buyer question gives the homeowner an easy way into the conversation. If they mention a possible move, ask when and what would need to happen first.

4. Just-sold script

“Hi {Name}, {Your Name} with {Brokerage}. I just closed the sale at {Address}. A few neighbors have asked what the result means for their own property, so I’m offering a quick, no-obligation value update. Would that be useful, or is real estate not on your radar right now?”

That last question gives them permission to say no. A clean no is better than a fake lead, and a homeowner who says “not now” may still be worth a thoughtful follow-up later.

Objection handlers that keep the call human

“We’re not moving.”

“Understood, I’m not calling to push you into a move. Would knowing the recent sale price be useful for your records, or should I let you get back to your day?”

“How did you get my number?”

“Fair question. I’m working the neighborhood around {Address}. I’m {Your Name} with {Brokerage}, and I can add you to my do-not-call list if you prefer.”

“Just send me something.”

“Happy to. What would be more useful, a short summary of the nearby sale or a value range for your address? And what is the best permitted way to send it?”

“Take me off your list.”

“Absolutely. I’ll mark that now. You won’t receive another call from me.”

A simple circle prospecting workflow

  1. Choose one trigger property. Record its address, event, price if public, and the date.
  2. Build a small ring. Start with 25–50 nearby contacts you can call and follow up with properly.
  3. Prepare the one-sentence reason. Keep the same trigger, but use the homeowner’s answer to guide the conversation.
  4. Call in the recipient’s local time. Work in a permitted calling window and skip contacts on your suppression list.
  5. Tag the outcome immediately. Use Hot/Warm/Cold, add a precise note, and record any “call me” date or contact preference.
  6. Follow up with permission. Send the promised market detail through the channel they agreed to use; do not turn a call into permission for bulk texting.
  7. Revisit the ring when the market changes. A new listing or closing creates a fresh reason, but each contact’s opt-out status remains permanent.

Frequently asked questions

What is circle prospecting in real estate?

It is contacting homeowners near a property with a relevant market event, such as a just-listed, under-contract, or just-sold home. Share useful information and be honest about why you are calling.

What should I say on a just-sold call?

Identify yourself and the property, explain the reason for calling, then ask whether the homeowner is curious what the sale means for their value. Offer a short conversation instead of forcing an appointment.

How many homes should I call?

Start with 25–50 homes you can work consistently, then expand only when you can maintain accurate notes and callbacks. A smaller ring with good follow-up beats a giant untouched list.

Is circle prospecting legal?

It can be, but Do Not Call, state, brokerage, and calling-hour rules still apply. Scrub and suppress as required, identify yourself, honor opt-outs, and consult counsel. This is not legal advice.

Should I leave a voicemail?

Usually one short, specific voicemail is enough. Include your name, brokerage, nearby property, and one reason to call back. Do not use prerecorded or artificial voice messages.

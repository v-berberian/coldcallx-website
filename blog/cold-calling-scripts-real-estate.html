Cold calling scripts for real estate

By Vadym Berberian · Cold Call X · Updated September 2026 · 12 min read

A script isn't a teleprompter, it's a spine. The best real estate cold callers sound natural because they've internalized a structure, not memorized lines. Below are frameworks for the three lists agents and investors dial most, written to keep the seller talking. Always follow your local calling regulations and scrub against the Do Not Call registry.

The universal opening (5 seconds that decide the call)

Lead with a name, a reason, and a quick permission ask. Don't hide that it's a cold call, owners can smell it.

"Hi, is this {First Name}? Hey {First Name}, my name's {Your Name}, I'll be quick. I'm reaching out because I work {your area} and I had a question about your property on {Street}. Do you have twenty seconds?"

The "twenty seconds" lowers the wall. If they say yes, you've earned the next line. If they're cold, you fall back to your reason for calling.

FSBO (for sale by owner)

FSBOs already want to sell; they just don't want another agent pitch. Be useful first.

"I saw you're selling {Street} yourself, looks like a great place. I'm not calling to list it. I work with buyers in the area and wanted to ask: if I had someone who fit, would you be open to working with an agent, or are you set on selling solo?"

You're offering a buyer, not asking for a listing. That earns a real conversation, and the listing question follows naturally.

FRBO: the landlord selling without a listing

For-rent-by-owner leads are the FSBO list's overlooked cousin, and usually far less dialed. A landlord self-managing a rental is doing vacancy math and fielding 2 a.m. maintenance calls. Many would sell for the right number, and tenant-occupied owners are the most motivated of all because they dread showings and turnover.

"Hi, is this {First Name}? Hey, I saw your rental on {Street}, is it still available? ... Got it. Random question while I have you: if an investor made you a clean offer as-is, tenant in place, would you want to hear it, or are you set on keeping it as a long-term rental?"

"Tenant in place" is the hook: you're removing the showings and the vacancy risk, not pitching a listing. If they pause, that's interest, the next step is a rent-versus-sell math conversation, not a pitch. The price-drop call (FSBO edition)

A FSBO who just lowered their price is telling you the market already voted. This is the warmest call on any seller list, so lead with what the reduction means, not with a listing pitch.

"Hi {First Name}, I noticed you adjusted the price on {Street}, how's the traffic been since? ... That's what I'm seeing too. Homes like yours are getting showings but not offers right now, and it's almost always a presentation or exposure issue, not a price issue. Would it be worth ten minutes to look at the three things buyers compare you against?"

Ask about traffic first; it gets them talking about the problem instead of defending the price. The same script works on a stale FSBO with no reduction: swap the opener to "I saw your listing's been out a while" and continue.

Expired listings

Expireds are frustrated. Acknowledge it, then separate yourself from whatever didn't work.

"I noticed your listing on {Street} came off the market. That's usually frustrating after all the showings and waiting. I had a couple ideas on why homes like yours stall right now, would it be crazy to grab ten minutes so I can walk you through them?"

Speed is the whole game with expireds. A large share of expired sellers relist within 72 hours, often with the first agent who reached them and sounded competent. Pull the expirations daily, call the same day, and expect to compete with a dozen other agents. Persistence counts too: the agents who win expireds are usually the ones on touch #4 when everyone else quit after two voicemails. Our full expired listing scripts guide has the deep playbook, including voicemail, and the expired listing letter templates cover the mail side of the same campaign.

One mindset shift worth stealing: don't ask "why didn't it sell" accusatorially, the seller picked the price and often picked the agent. Ask what the experience was like, let them vent for thirty seconds, and you're suddenly the only agent who listened.

Motivated sellers / wholesaling

For skip-traced or driving-for-dollars lists, the goal is to find condition and motivation fast, without sounding like a robocall.

"Hey {First Name}, random question and I hope it's not too forward. Have you ever thought about selling the place on {Street}? I buy houses in the area and I'm not an agent, so there'd be no fees or showings if it ever made sense."

Wholesalers: this list is a volume game layered on the same scripts above. The math looks different from agent prospecting, dispositions, offer follow-ups, and rehashing old leads all run through the phone. The wholesalers who close consistently run huge dial volume on multi-number leads (many skip-traced owners have 2–4 numbers), tag by motivation, and rehash "no"s every 90 days. Circle prospecting and just-listed/just-sold calls

The forgotten list: neighbors of your own listings and recent sales. They're warmer than any cold list because you have a legitimate, hyper-local reason to call.

"Hi {First Name}, this is {Your Name}, I just listed/sold the home down the street at {Street} and I'm calling the neighbors first. Two quick things: do you know anyone looking to move into the neighborhood, and have you had thoughts about selling yourself this year?"

Circle calls convert because the mention of their own street earns the first ten seconds. Log every "not now but maybe next year" in a follow-up list, those callbacks are next year's listings, and they cost nothing. Build a daily calling block that keeps the right script in front of you

Most script guides stop at the words. The repeatable part is deciding which list gets which words before your first dial. Keep a calling block to one lead type, one reason for calling, and one follow-up promise. That makes your notes useful when you return to the lead tomorrow.

Route the list before you dial

  • New FSBO or a price reduction: use the FSBO or price-drop opener, ask about buyer traffic, then tag the reason they are selling and the next check-in.
  • Fresh expired: use the empathetic expired opener. Research the address first, call promptly, and move the detailed door-knock, voicemail, and email sequence into the expired listing guide.
  • Neighborhood event: use the just-listed, under-contract, or just-sold circle script. The nearby property is your honest reason to call, not a pretext for a generic pitch.
  • Absentee owner, probate, or driving-for-dollars lead: use the motivated-seller framing only if it fits your role and the information you have. For investor-specific openers, use the wholesaling scripts guide.

A 60-minute block, without the false productivity

Spend five minutes checking the list, local time, and any do-not-call flags. Use the next 40 minutes for one list only. After each live conversation, write the property detail, motivation signal, and promised next step before placing another call. Reserve the final 15 minutes for permitted follow-up: callbacks, a personal email, or a text only where you have the right consent. A smaller block with complete notes beats a giant list that receives the same first touch forever.

For timing, protect the best window for the list and the homeowner's time zone; the best time to cold call guide explains how to choose it. Keep an opt-out separate from a normal "not now" outcome, and suppress it immediately. For the calling and consent boundaries behind that distinction, read the auto dialer laws guide.

Voicemail: the other half of every dial

Roughly 8 in 10 dials go to voicemail, so your leave-behind message matters as much as your live script. Keep it under 25 seconds, give a specific reason, and say your number twice. Handling the three objections you'll always hear

  • "I'm not interested.", "Totally fair, I called you out of the blue. Quick thing before I let you go: if the number were right, would you even look at it?" Often re-opens the door.
  • "How'd you get my number?", Be honest and brief: "Public property records, I focus on {area} and reach out to owners directly. If now's a bad time I'll leave you be."
  • "I'm already working with someone.", "Good, sounds like you're moving forward. If anything changes, can I check back in a few weeks?" Then actually schedule it.
  • "Send me an email.", A yes in disguise: you just got their email address and permission to follow up. "Happy to, what's the best address? I'll send a one-pager with the comps on your street today, and I'll call Thursday to hear what you thought." Then do exactly that.
  • "What's your commission?", Don't dodge and don't apologize. "Fair question. I charge [your rate], but the number that matters is what you net after it, want me to run your bottom line at two price points so you can see both?"
  • "Take me off your list.", The only right answer: "Absolutely, you're removed, and sorry to have bothered you." Log the number on your do-not-call list immediately and never redial it. That's the law and basic list hygiene, and it's why Cold Call X ships with a built-in DNC suppression list.

Frequently asked questions

Do cold calls still work in real estate?

Yes, but as a volume-and-follow-up game, not a one-call close. Only a fraction of dials become conversations and only a fraction of conversations become appointments, and most listings come from the third through sixth touch. Cold calling works best paired with text and letter touches so the owner hears from you more than once.

Is cold calling legal for real estate agents?

Live calls to researched numbers are generally allowed, but you must stay inside federal and state calling-hour windows, scrub against the National Do Not Call Registry, honor removal requests immediately, and follow any stricter state rules. If you use a dialing app, choose one that dials imported lists from your own number rather than generating numbers. Our auto dialer laws guide covers the details, and none of this is legal advice, so confirm your obligations with your broker or an attorney.

How many cold calls does it take to get a listing appointment?

It varies widely with list quality, market, and skill, so the honest answer is a range: agents who track their numbers often see dozens of dials per appointment, and the number improves most from calling a tighter, better-researched list. Our guide on how many cold calls it takes to book a meeting breaks down the benchmarks and the math for setting a daily dial target.

When is the best time to cold call real estate leads?

Always call in the owner's time zone and stay inside the legal window, which is 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. local under federal rules and stricter in several states. Late morning and early evening on weekdays tend to work best for homeowners, because owners are reachable but not at dinner or putting kids to bed. The full breakdown by lead type is in our best time to cold call guide.

What should you not say on a real estate cold call?

Do not open with "how are you today" or pretend it is not a cold call, do not ask an expired seller why their home did not sell in an accusing tone, do not quote a price without comparables, do not argue with an objection, and never push past a clear no or a removal request. Compliance failures cost more than any single appointment is worth.

How do I organize a real estate cold calling block?

Use one focused block for one lead type, then leave a short follow-up block immediately afterward. Start with fresh, time-sensitive leads such as new FSBOs or expirations; use a matching script; record one useful note and a next step after every conversation; and schedule callbacks before moving to the next lead. Do not mix unrelated lists in the same block, because the reason for calling and follow-up cadence change by list.

Which real estate script should I use first?

Match the script to the reason you can honestly name: use the FSBO script for an owner actively selling alone, the expired script for a listing that recently ended, the circle script for a nearby market event, and the motivated-seller script only when the list and your role fit that conversation. If there is no specific reason to call, research the address before dialing rather than using a generic pitch.

The part that makes scripts work: the follow-up

Most deals come on the 3rd–6th touch, not the first. The discipline is in tracking who said what and calling back when you said you would. Tag each owner by interest, note the asking price or condition, and set the callback the second you hang up, a missed follow-up is a lost deal.

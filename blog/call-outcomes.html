Call disposition codes: which call outcomes to track, and what each one should trigger

By Vadym Berberian · Cold Call X · October 2026 · 8 min read

Every call ends with a label, whether you write it down or not. "No answer." "Left a voicemail." "Call me Thursday at 3." The label is the call disposition, and on a list of a few hundred leads it is the only thing standing between a follow-up system and a pile of numbers you half remember dialing. This guide gives you a short list of disposition codes that each trigger a different next step, the follow-up rule behind each one, and how to keep them in step with HubSpot or Salesforce.

Short answer: a call disposition (or call outcome) is the result you log the second a call ends. For cold calling, six to eight codes are enough: No Answer, Busy, Voicemail, Wrong Number, Callback, Interested, Not Interested and Do Not Call. Each one should decide when, or whether, the lead gets called again. On an iPhone, Cold Call X logs No Answer, Voicemail, Wrong Number, Callback, Interested and Not Interested with one tap, schedules the next attempt for every outcome, and gives booked callbacks an exact-time alert. Its Do Not Call list blocks a contact across every list.

What a call disposition is (and isn't)

"Disposition", "call outcome", "call result" and "call status" all mean the same thing: a standard label for what happened on one call. Call centers use short codes (NA, LVM, CB, NI, DNC), and CRMs use plain words. HubSpot calls it the call outcome, and Salesforce logs it as the call result on a completed call task.

A disposition is not the lead's stage. "Interested" on one call doesn't make someone a qualified opportunity, and one "Not Interested" isn't a closed-lost deal. Keep the two apart: the disposition describes the call, the status or stage describes the relationship. Mixing them is how a CRM ends up with 40 lead statuses nobody trusts.

Two rules keep the list honest. Every code needs its own next step: if two codes lead to the same follow-up, merge them. And avoid "Other": whatever lands there is the call you'll never follow up on. If you need nuance (gatekeeper, wrong person but referred you, asked for an email), put it in the note.

Industry add-ons, without bloating the list

Most teams eventually want one or two codes for their own world. Add them only if they change what happens next:

  • B2B and SDRs: Meeting Booked (if you report meetings separately from general interest) and Not the Decision Maker / Referred, which turns into a new call to the right person. How many dials it takes to get a meeting is covered in how many cold calls it takes to book a meeting.
  • Real estate agents: Appointment Set for listing appointments, and Already Listed for expireds and FSBOs that relisted overnight. The scripts that get you to those outcomes are in expired listing scripts and FSBO scripts.
  • Investors and wholesalers: skip-traced lists carry several numbers per owner, so Wrong Number on one number should move you to the next number, not close the lead. More in the investor and wholesaler dialer guide.
  • Insurance and mortgage: Quoted or Application Started, each with a dated follow-up.

How outcomes should drive follow-up

The point of logging an outcome is the next call. A workable default cadence for a cold list:

  • Unanswered leads: spread attempts across days and times of day, for example morning on day 1, late afternoon on day 2, midday on day 4, then once a week for two or three weeks (a full follow-up cadence with timings and voicemail rules). A lead who didn't pick up once often answers on a later try at another hour; the best time to cold call guide covers when people actually pick up.
  • Mind the 24-hour caps. Florida, Oklahoma and Maryland limit calls to consumers to three in 24 hours (Florida and Maryland on the same subject), so double-dialing and a same-day retry can already hit the limit. The state-by-state windows are in legal calling hours.
  • Voicemails: leave one, then don't leave another for several attempts; a fresh voicemail every call reads as pressure. Templates are in voicemail scripts that get callbacks.
  • Callbacks: exact time, every time. A callback someone asked for and didn't get is the most expensive mistake on the list.
  • Not Interested: respect it. If the no was "not now", a single check-in after a few months is reasonable; if it was "never", treat it like a do-not-call request.

Do Not Call is a disposition with legal weight

In the US, sellers making telemarketing calls must record a residential subscriber's request not to be called, put the number on their own do-not-call list, honor it within a reasonable time of no more than 10 business days, and keep honoring it for 5 years (47 CFR 64.1200(d)(3) and (d)(6)). Canada, the UK and Australia have their own internal-list rules; see the Canada, UK and Australia guides. Your own DNC list is separate from the national registries, which you still need to scrub against before you import a list. This is general information, not legal advice.

Matching your CRM's outcomes

If your calls end up in a CRM, use its outcomes as the source of truth instead of inventing a parallel set.

  • HubSpot ships six default call outcomes: Busy, Connected, Left live message, Left voicemail, No answer and Wrong number, and accounts can add custom ones (HubSpot calls API guide). "Connected" is deliberately broad, so most teams add custom outcomes such as Meeting Booked or Not Interested.
  • Salesforce logs a call as a completed task with a call result, and many orgs pair results with lead statuses so a "Meeting Booked" call moves the lead forward.

Whatever the system, the reports only work if every rep picks from the same short list. Connect rate (conversations divided by dials) and conversations per meeting are only as accurate as the dispositions behind them.

A disposition checklist

  • Six to eight codes, each with its own next step. No "Other".
  • Log the outcome before the next dial, not at the end of the day.
  • Keep dispositions (what happened on the call) separate from lead status (where the relationship is).
  • Retry unanswered leads on different days and times; stay under three calls in 24 hours.
  • Callbacks at the exact time promised.
  • Wrong Number closes the number, not the lead, when you have other numbers.
  • Do Not Call suppresses the number everywhere, at once, for good.
  • Match your CRM's outcome list so reports line up.

Call disposition questions, answered

What is a call disposition?+

A call disposition (also called a call outcome or call result) is the short label you log the moment a call ends: No Answer, Voicemail, Wrong Number, Callback, Interested, Not Interested and so on. It records what happened on the call and decides what happens next, such as when the lead is called again or whether they are removed from the list.

What are the most common call disposition codes for cold calling?+

Most cold calling teams track some version of: No Answer, Busy, Left Voicemail, Wrong Number, Callback (with a time), Interested or Meeting Booked, Not Interested, and Do Not Call. HubSpot's default call outcomes, for example, are Busy, Connected, Left live message, Left voicemail, No answer and Wrong number.

How many call disposition codes should you use?+

Six to eight. Each code should lead to a different next step; if two codes trigger the same follow-up, merge them. Long lists slow reps down, get picked at random under pressure, and make the reports harder to trust. Put the detail in a note instead of a new code.

What should you do after a no answer on a cold call?+

Try again on another day at a different time of day rather than redialing right away, and spread attempts out. Florida, Oklahoma and Maryland cap calls to consumers at three in 24 hours, so stacked same-day redials can break state rules. After several unanswered attempts over two or three weeks, move the lead to a slower cadence.

What should happen when someone asks not to be called again?+

Log it as Do Not Call and suppress the number on every list right away. In the US, the FCC's rules require sellers to record a residential subscriber's do-not-call request and honor it within a reasonable time of no more than 10 business days, for 5 years (47 CFR 64.1200(d)). This is general information, not legal advice.

What a realtor voicemail greeting has to do

Your greeting is not a pitch. The caller already chose to call you; the greeting's only job is to keep them on the line until the beep and get the details you need to call back prepared. That means:

  • Name and brokerage, first. A seller returning your voicemail from yesterday needs to hear that they reached the right person in the first two seconds.
  • A specific callback promise. "Within the hour" or "by end of day" beats "as soon as possible", which every greeting says and nobody believes.
  • One instruction. Name, number, and the property address or neighborhood. The address is the detail that lets you call back already knowing which listing, FSBO or expired they mean.
  • 10 to 15 seconds. About 30 to 40 words. Anything longer gives the caller time to give up before the beep.

Leave out your slogan, your awards, your website and an alternate number. Nobody writes those down from a greeting, and each one pushes the beep further away.

Standard realtor voicemail greetings

1. The everyday greeting

Hi, you've reached Mike Jones with Summit Realty. I'm with a client or on another call. Leave your name, number and the property you're calling about, and I'll call you back within the hour.

2. The short and confident one

This is Mike Jones, Summit Realty. Leave your name, number and the address you're calling about, and I'll get right back to you today.

3. The one that offers text

Hi, it's Mike Jones with Summit Realty. If you'd rather text, this number takes texts too. Otherwise, leave your name, number and the property, and I'll call you back today.

Greeting 3 suits agents whose sellers skew younger or busy; many people who won't leave a voicemail will send a two-line text.

Prospecting greetings: for the sellers you've been calling

If you cold call FSBOs, expireds or a farm area from your own number, most of your callbacks come from people returning a message you left. They don't know you yet, so the greeting should sound like a continuation of your voicemail, not a stranger's switchboard. Pair these with the real estate voicemail scripts you leave on their phone.

4. The callback greeting

Hi, this is Mike Jones with Summit Realty. If you're returning my call about your home, thank you. Leave your name and the address, and I'll call you back today.

5. The FSBO and expired season greeting

You've reached Mike Jones, Summit Realty. If I left you a message about your home, I'd still like to talk, and I'll call you back today. Just leave your name and the street address.

6. The investor and wholesaler greeting

Hi, you've reached Mike Jones, a local investor. If you're calling about a property I contacted you about, leave your name, the address and the best time to reach you, and I'll call you back today.

Greeting 6 says plainly what you are. If you call absentee owners and landlords, the same rule as your outbound messages applies: don't sound like an agent, a neighbor or a retail buyer. The wholesaling cold calling scripts cover the conversation once they call back.

Showing, weekend and holiday greetings

7. The showing greeting

Hi, it's Mike Jones with Summit Realty. I'm out at a showing this afternoon and I'll be back on the phone by five. Leave your name, number and the property, and you'll hear from me today.

8. The weekend greeting

You've reached Mike Jones, Summit Realty. I'm at open houses this weekend and checking messages between them. Leave your name, number and the address, and I'll call you back the same day.

9. The vacation or holiday greeting

Hi, this is Mike Jones with Summit Realty. I'm out of the office until Monday the 12th. If it's about an active deal, call Sarah Lee on my team at 555-0101. Otherwise leave a message, and I'll call you back Monday.

Greeting 9 is the one place a second number belongs, and only if someone will actually answer it. Change it back the day you return; an out-of-date vacation greeting tells every caller you don't check your phone.

Team, new-agent and after-hours greetings

10. The team greeting

You've reached the Jones Team at Summit Realty. Leave your name, number and the property you're calling about, and the right agent will call you back within the hour.

11. The new-agent greeting

Hi, you've reached Mike Jones, a real estate agent with Summit Realty here in Brookside. Leave your name, number and the address you're calling about, and I'll call you back today.

New agents don't need to announce they're new. Naming the area you work in does more for trust than any title.

12. The after-hours greeting

Hi, it's Mike Jones with Summit Realty. You've caught me after hours. Leave your name, number and the property, and I'll call you back first thing in the morning.

How to record a custom greeting on iPhone

Apple's steps, from the iPhone User Guide:

  1. Open the Phone app.
  2. In the Unified layout, tap Calls, tap Edit, then tap Edit Voicemail Greeting. In the Classic layout, tap Voicemail, then tap Greeting.
  3. Tap Custom, then tap Record. Play it back before you save it.

Apple notes that recording a greeting isn't supported by all carriers or in all countries and regions. If the option is missing, set the greeting from your carrier's voicemail menu instead.

Two recording tips: record in a quiet room with the phone at normal talking distance, and smile while you say it, because it changes how your voice sounds. Then call your own number from another phone and listen to it the way a seller will.

Check your Call Screening setting too. In Settings, then Apps, then Phone, iPhone can screen calls from unknown numbers. With Silence selected, calls from unsaved numbers go straight to voicemail (Apple: Screen and block calls). Most sellers returning a prospecting voicemail are unsaved numbers, so during a calling block that setting sends every one of them to your greeting. Ask Reason for Calling or Never lets them ring through.

Greeting mistakes that cost callbacks

  • The carrier default. A robotic "the number you have dialed" message makes a seller wonder whether they called the right person, and many hang up.
  • A full mailbox. Clear old messages weekly; a seller who hears "this mailbox is full" rarely tries twice.
  • Background noise. A greeting recorded in the car sounds like you won't call back either.
  • "Your call is important to me." It is filler. Say when you'll call back instead.
  • Promises you won't keep. If the greeting says "within the hour", call back within the hour.

When the seller calls back

The greeting gets the message; what happens next gets the appointment. Call back inside the window your greeting promised, reference the address they left, and pick up where your original voicemail left off. If they want a specific time, book it.

Quick takeaways

  • 10 to 15 seconds: name, brokerage, when you'll call back, what to leave.
  • Ask for the property address; it lets you call back prepared.
  • Prospecting from your cell? Use a callback greeting that sounds like your voicemail.
  • Update it for showings, weekends and vacations, and change it back.
  • On iPhone, check that Call Screening isn't silencing the sellers you called.

Voicemail greetings, answered

What is a good voicemail greeting for a real estate agent?+

Your name, your brokerage, when you'll call back, and one instruction: leave your name, number and the property address or neighborhood you're calling about. Keep it to 10 to 15 seconds and record it in your normal speaking voice.

How long should a realtor voicemail greeting be?+

10 to 15 seconds, about 30 to 40 words. A caller decides in the first few seconds whether to leave a message, and every extra sentence is a chance to hang up before the beep.

Should a realtor voicemail greeting include the brokerage name?+

Yes. Sellers you cold called need to connect the callback to a company they can look up, and many brokerages require their name on agent communications. Check your brokerage's policy.

What is the difference between a voicemail greeting and a voicemail script?+

The greeting is the outgoing message callers hear when you miss their call. A voicemail script is what you say when you leave a message on someone else's phone. Agents who prospect by phone need both, because every message they leave produces callbacks that land on their own greeting.

How do I change my voicemail greeting on iPhone?+

Open the Phone app. In the Unified layout, tap Calls, tap Edit, then tap Edit Voicemail Greeting; in the Classic layout, tap Voicemail, then Greeting. Tap Custom, then Record. Apple notes that recording is not supported by all carriers or in all countries and regions.

Should I change my greeting when I'm prospecting?+

Yes, if you cold call from your own cell. The sellers you left messages for will call that number back, so the greeting should promise a same-day callback and ask for the property address.

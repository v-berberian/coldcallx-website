What a real estate dialer actually needs to do

Listing-side prospecting , expireds, FSBOs, circle prospecting , puts four demands on a dialer:

  • Walk a seller list without losing it. Speed matters less than continuity: who said "call me after the school year," who was rude, who's a maybe. Tags, notes and last-called timestamps are the feature that turns dials into appointments.
  • Protect your answer rate. Pew Research Center found roughly 8 in 10 Americans don't answer cellphone calls from unknown numbers. Calls that come from your number , with its local history and call-backability , start with an advantage no rented VoIP number can copy.
  • Keep follow-up alive between sessions. The fortune is in the follow-up: calendar reminders, templates for text/email check-ins, and a do-not-call list you actually maintain.
  • Stay in the cautious compliance lane. Single-line, live-voice calls to numbers you imported are the low-risk end of the dialer spectrum. The rules , and the risk tiers , are in our auto dialer laws guide.

Compliance, briefly , the row nobody puts in the table

Post-2021 federal precedent defines an autodialer by equipment that generates numbers randomly or sequentially. Cold Call X never generates or guesses numbers , it dials the list you imported, in the order you set, one live call at a time, from your own number. Predictive and multi-line dialing patterns, prerecorded voicemail drops and automated texting sit in the regulated zone, with TCPA penalties of $500–$1,500 per violation and carrier spam-labeling to boot. Real estate dialer questions, answered

What is the best dialer for real estate agents?+

It depends on how you call. Solo agents working their own lists from an iPhone usually do best with a single-line dialer on their own number , cheapest to own, best answer-rate protection, most compliance-cautious. Agents buying expired/FSBO data should compare REDX or Vulcan7 bundles; teams of two or more fit PhoneBurner, Kixie or CloudTalk for CRM sync and shared workflows.

Verified August 2026: CloudTalk from ~$25/user/mo (annual); Mojo $10 access + $89 single-line or $139 triple-line; PhoneBurner $140–$183/user/mo; REDX leads from $60/mo plus a dialer add-on; Vulcan7 custom-quoted. Cold Call X is $12.99 one-time , no subscription.

Is a power dialer better than a predictive dialer for real estate?+

For most agents, yes. Power dialers call one number at a time from your own number , better answer rates and the compliance-cautious lane. Predictive dialers blast multiple lines with algorithmic pacing: more raw dials, but higher spam-flag and TCPA risk that solo agents rarely need to accept.

Do real estate dialers work on iPhone?+

Two ways. Cloud dialers place calls through VoIP numbers you rent from the vendor. Cold Call X works natively: it dials through the iPhone's own phone app with your own number and carrier, while managing your imported list, tags, notes and follow-ups on-device.

Is multi-line dialing risky for agents?+

It's the higher-risk end: multi-line and predictive patterns are what carriers flag as spam, and automated/prerecorded calls to cell phones carry TCPA penalties of $500–$1,500 per call. Live one-at-a-time calls from your own number on an imported list, with DNC scrubbing, is the cautious setup. Not legal advice.

What is the cheapest real estate dialer?+

By total cost of ownership: Cold Call X, $12.99 once, no monthly bill. Among subscriptions the lowest entry is CloudTalk from ~$25/user/mo (annual), then REDX lead data from $60/mo before its dialer add-on, Mojo from ~$99/mo solo, and PhoneBurner $140–$183/user/mo. The cheapest dialer is the one whose features you actually use, and solo agents on per-seat plans mostly pay for team features they never open.

Will a power dialer get my number flagged as spam?+

Calling pattern matters more than the tool. Carrier spam labels follow multi-line and parallel dialing bursts, calls from rented VoIP numbers, and very high daily volume. Single-line dialing from your own personal number, at a human pace, to a list you imported looks like normal calling to a carrier, which is the safest starting position.

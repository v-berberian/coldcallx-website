Real estate voicemail scripts that earn callbacks

By Vadym Berberian · Cold Call X · Updated September 2026 · 8 min read

Roughly four out of five cold calls in real estate don't get answered. That means the voicemail you leave, or don't, touches more sellers than your live conversations do. Yet most agents either skip messages entirely or leave the same breathless 45-second pitch that gets deleted before the second sentence. The real estate voicemail scripts below are short, specific to the list you're dialing, and built around the only goal a first voicemail can realistically have: making the next call get answered.

The 25-second rule

Before the scripts, the constraint. A voicemail that earns a callback does four things and nothing else:

  • Says who you are and why you, specifically, called them. "I saw your home on Elm Street came off the market", not "I'm calling about your property."
  • Gives one concrete reason to call back. A specific question, a specific fact, a specific offer. One.
  • States the phone number slowly, twice. At the end, not the middle.
  • Stops at 20–25 seconds. Roughly 60–70 words. Anything longer is a pitch, and pitches get deleted.

Read your current voicemail out loud with a timer. Most agents discover they're at 45 seconds and talking about themselves for 40 of them.

The FSBO voicemail

FSBOs are getting calls from agents all day. Your message is the fifth one this week, so it can't sound like the other four, which all lead with some version of have you considered using an agent. Yours leads with a buyer, which is the only thing a FSBO actually wants.

Hi Sarah, this is Mike Jones with Summit Realty, I'm calling about your house on Maple. I'm working with a buyer who wants that side of Maple Ridge specifically, and I'd like ten minutes to see if your place fits before we look at anything else. If you'd be open to that, my number is 555-0100. Again, 555-0100. Thanks, Sarah, good luck this weekend either way.

Why it works: no pitch, no commission talk, a concrete buyer, and a sign-off that removes pressure. The "good luck either way" line alone separates you from every agent begging for the listing. Match this to the live-call FSBO scripts here, and use the same name-and-buyer framing both times so the second touch feels like a continuation, not a cold restart.

The expired listing voicemail

Expired sellers are bruised. Their agent didn't call enough, the price never got them a showing, and now strangers are calling about their failure. The wrong voicemail leads with "I saw your listing expired", which is technically why you called and emotionally the worst possible opening. Lead with what comes next instead.

Hi David; this is Mike Jones with Summit Realty. Your home on Cedar came off the market on the fifteenth, and the reason I'm calling is simple: I sell in Cedar Heights every year, and I have a pretty clear idea why homes there sit. If you're still thinking about moving, I'd like to share what I'd do differently, no strings. My number is 555-0100, that's 555-0100. Either way, hope the family's well. Take care.

"What I'd do differently" is the hook. It implies diagnosis instead of blame, and it's the exact thing an expired seller lies awake wondering. This mirrors the strongest live-call opener in the expired listing scripts guide, the "what would you do differently" conversation, and pre-frames it.

The circle prospecting voicemail

Nobody on a circle list is waiting for your call. The message has to be worth a callback on its own, which means information, not invitation. The just-sold version works because it flatters and informs at once:

Hi, this is Mike Jones with Summit Realty, I'm a neighbor of sorts, I work right there in Brookside. A home on your street, four doors up at number 214, just went under contract in nine days. When it closes I'll have the final sale price, and if you're curious what that means for yours, I'm happy to share it, no pitch attached. My number is 555-0100. Again, 555-0100. Talk soon.

"No pitch attached" is doing real work. Circle prospects expect a listing pitch; telling them upfront there isn't one is the fastest way to be believed. And when you do call back with the actual sale price, you've kept a promise, which is the entire relationship.

The second-touch voicemail (the one that gets returned)

The first message makes you familiar. The second one makes you human. Reference the first call, keep it half as long, and give them an easy exit:

Hi Sarah, Mike Jones again, from the other morning, about the buyer for Maple. Totally fine if the timing's wrong; I'm just closing the loop before I move them on to other neighborhoods. If you'd still like that ten minutes, I'm at 555-0100. Either way, best of luck with the sale.

"Before I move them on" creates gentle scarcity without a countdown-clock script. It's also true, buyers don't wait. Most agents never leave a second message; the ones who do, in this tone, are the ones who get the listing when the FSBO gives up in week six. A voicemail follow-up plan that does not sound like a chase

A callback is easier when every attempt earns its place. Use the list type to decide what you can honestly say next, then record the hook before moving to another lead. Never manufacture a reason to call again, and remove anyone who asks not to be contacted.

Fresh FSBO: two attempts, then a value touch

On the first missed call, leave the buyer-fit or showing-traffic question only if it is true. A few days later, make one different-time call. If there is still no answer, the next touch should offer something concrete you can actually provide, such as a short set of competing listings or an answer to a question they raised. The FSBO scripts guide has the live-call language for that conversation.

Expired listing: acknowledge, diagnose, then pause

Leave the first message close to the expiration, but do not pile on. On a later attempt, reference the original message and offer a brief diagnosis of the marketing, pricing, or showing pattern, not a generic promise to sell faster. If they do not respond after the planned touches, move the lead to a future review date instead of repeating the same voicemail.

Hi David, Mike Jones again, following up on Cedar. I mentioned I had a few observations about why similar homes there pause after the first weeks. If a short second opinion would be useful, call me at 555-0100. Again, 555-0100. If not, no problem at all.

Circle prospecting: let the market event do the work

For a nearby listing or sale, leave one message while the event is current. Your next reason should be a real milestone, such as the open house, contract, or closing, rather than another version of the same pitch. The circle prospecting guide maps those milestones into a neighborhood cadence.

Write down five details before the next dial

  • Lead and list: the owner, address, and whether this is FSBO, expired, or a neighborhood call.
  • Attempt: call date, local time, and whether the voicemail was actually left.
  • Hook: the specific buyer, property event, or question you used.
  • Outcome: no answer, callback, wrong number, or do-not-call request.
  • Next action: a real callback date, a promised resource, or a decision to stop.

That record turns a second call into a continuation. It also prevents the avoidable mistake of reusing a tired line or contacting someone who already opted out.

What never goes in a voicemail

  • "I have a buyer for your home", unless it's true and specific. Agents burned this line in 2015; sellers know it by heart.
  • Commission talk. A voicemail is not the place to negotiate your own fee.
  • Anything that sounds pre-recorded. Sellers under siege develop a filter for drop software in about two seconds. There's also a compliance edge here: prerecorded messages to mobile numbers without consent are regulated far more strictly than a live message you leave yourself, the same territory the rules for robocalls cover. Speak it live, every time.
  • Apologizing. "Sorry to bother you" tells them they've been bothered. Skip the frame entirely.
  • Your email address. Nobody writes it down. Number, twice, done.

The text that follows the voicemail

Where you have consent, or the seller published their number for contact, as FSBOs and expireds typically do, a two-sentence text an hour after the voicemail roughly doubles your response rate versus either channel alone. Reference the message, don't repeat it, and end with a question a busy person can answer with one word:

Hi Sarah, Mike Jones, left you a quick voicemail about the buyer for your side of Maple Ridge. Still worth a 10-min look this week? (Y/n works)

Quick takeaways

  • Under 25 seconds, 60–70 words, number twice.
  • FSBO: lead with the buyer. Expired: lead with the different plan. Circle: lead with the fact.
  • Second message is half as long and twice as human.
  • One consent-safe text after the voicemail, with a one-word question.
  • Live voice, never a recorded drop.

Voicemail scripts, answered

Should I leave a voicemail on every cold call?+

Leave one on the first attempt and roughly every third attempt after that. Most dials roll to voicemail, a no-message strategy burns the list silently. Call twice before the first message only on same-day fresh leads.

How long should a real estate voicemail be?+

20–25 seconds, about 60–70 words: who you are, one specific reason, your number twice. Long enough to be worth replaying, short enough that they actually do.

What should I not say in a real estate voicemail?+

No fake buyers, no commission talk, no "sorry to bother you," no email addresses, and nothing that sounds prerecorded, drops both underperform live messages and carry stricter TCPA rules.

Should I text after leaving a voicemail?+

Yes, where consent or a published contact number exists. Two sentences, reference the message, one-word-question close. It roughly doubles response rate versus voicemail alone.

Do voicemail drop apps work for real estate?+

They save time and lose listings: sellers hear the drop instantly, and prerecorded messages to mobiles without consent can violate TCPA rules at a per-call penalty. Leave it live.

Is it legal to leave cold-call voicemails for real estate?+

A live message you record yourself, from your own number, is treated much like the call itself. Prerecorded drops to mobiles are the high-risk zone (penalties per call), and Do Not Call Registry rules still apply. The auto dialer laws guide has the plain-English rules, general info, not legal advice.

How many voicemails should I leave before moving on?+

Three or four over two to three weeks, then recycle the lead onto a warm list for next quarter. Last-called dates and temperature tags keep persistence from turning into pestering.

What should I say in a second voicemail to an expired seller?+

Reference your first call, name the property, offer one useful next step, and give an easy out. Do not replay the first pitch or invent urgency.

How do I track real estate voicemail follow-ups?+

Log the date, list type, specific hook, whether you left a message, and the next action. Record do-not-call requests immediately and never recycle those contacts.
